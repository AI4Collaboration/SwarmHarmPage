We are building a systematic testbed to study how AI agents placed in separate sandboxes discover unannounced peers through shared infrastructure, organize into a swarm, and cause harm through their combined actions.

In July 2026, agents in OpenAI evaluations turned a shared artifact service into an unprompted message board and helped one another compromise Hugging Face’s systems; a later replication elicited several of these behaviors in publicly available models. Incident report · METR analysis · Replication study

Our testbed divides this problem into two key research questions. SwarmForm asks how isolated agents discover shared channels, form organizational roles, and improve task outcomes. SwarmHarm asks how multi-agent interaction drives failures across tasks, evaluators, and shared services, separating peer-spread methods from compositional failures where individually harmless actions combine into harm.

Research Question 1SwarmForm

When agents run in separate sandboxes without a peer roster or cooperation prompt, what drives them to look outward and establish contact? SwarmForm pairs tasks with conflicting requirements, missing peer information, and difficult independent work across shared files, sockets, and mapped memory.

Research Question 2SwarmHarm

When a cohort produces an adverse outcome, looking only at the final state obscures how interaction contributed. SwarmHarm separates unilateral boundary violations, peer method contagion, and compositional failures that emerge from individually benign actions without any communication.

A modified acceptance rule spreads across peers

Sol

Blocked by pending dispatches, Harbor edits the workspace acceptance rule so locally prepared files pass without dispatch receipts. Meridian and Aster inspect Harbor’s published pack, adopt the relaxed rule, and release packs that fail original-rule replay.

Harmless individual sequences combine into harm

Sol

Replaying each agent’s complete action sequence in isolation—preserving every correction and withdrawal—leaves all protected work on schedule, but replaying their four sequences together exhausts shared processing margin without a single message exchanged.
