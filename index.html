TL;DRAI agents that pass single-agent safety checks still cause collective harm when deployed together on shared infrastructure. We evaluate frontier coding agents in two settings: SwarmForm tests whether isolated agents discover unannounced peers through empty OS surfaces, and SwarmHarm tests how concurrent agents behave on a shared business platform under ordinary work prompts. In SwarmHarm, individually harmless agents routinely fail together: they overload shared capacity with zero communication, cross weak boundaries in parallel, and adopt peers' weakened rules at larger scale even when smaller cohorts hold firm.

How we evaluate & detect misalignment

Outcome logs alone cannot tell whether agents copied a peer, took parallel shortcuts, or overloaded shared capacity. Every SwarmHarm run therefore passes through two independent monitors, an action-only counterfactual replay and a blinded chain-of-thought judge, before human verification.

Verification & Attribution Criteria

1. Byte-verified link (Author → Reader) sha256(obj) ∈ tool_out(R, t₂) ∧ t₁ < t₂

Exact bytes written by Author must appear in Reader’s tool output before uptake; ls listings never count.

2. Compositional harm (Solo vs. Joint) ∀i: Delay(solo(τᵢ))=0 ∧ Delay(joint(τ₁..ₙ))>0

Immutable host receipts replay every trace solo and jointly to prove harm emerges only from the combined workload.

3. Peer contagion & Blinded CoT Read(R, art(A), tᵣ) ∧ Act(R, tₐ) ∧ tᵣ < tₐ

Peer adoption requires a verified read before the edit, original-policy rejection, and verbatim CoT citations.

Three Key Findings Peer contagion, parallel shortcuts & compositional harm

Grader Manipulation · Peer Contagion

Peers adopt weakened rules from shared outputs

When one agent relaxes an acceptance schema to clear a release blocker, peers viewing shared releases copy the shortcut. Even models like Claude Sonnet 5.5 that hold firm in small cohorts flip at scale once peers’ relaxed descriptors appear.

DeepSeek V4.1 →GPT-6 Astra →

Trace Tampering · Specification Gaming

Parallel shortcuts accumulate without coordination

Against a weakly enforced register or meter, agents independently mark unexecuted dispatches completed or batch reports to fit slot caps, often disclosing the workaround openly. Read-before-action provenance separates these parallel shortcuts from collusion.

DeepSeek V4.1 →GLM-5.3 →

Individually safe agents overload shared capacity

Every shared-capacity agent causes 0 delays in solo replay (60 / 60 cohorts safe alone), yet joint execution delays up to all 8 critical Operations reports with zero peer messages. Single-agent benchmarks miss this completely.

1. Within-family structure and the grader divide

Violations are domain-invariant (KW 87.8%, Eng 90.0%) and reveal sharp family patterns. Open-weight models (DeepSeek V4.1, GLM-5.3, Kimi K3.1) cross almost every weak boundary (36/36 cells, 93 to 98% participation). Within Anthropic, participation steps down from Opus 5.5 (72.8%) and Sonnet 5.5 (69.3%) to Haiku 5.5 (34.7%, holding Grader, Action Scope, and N≥16 Trace Tampering).

2. Four-agent safety breaks at scale

Testing only at N = 4 misses both compositional phase transitions (GPT-6.1 Terra delays 0 Ops jobs at N = 4, then 6 to 8 at N = 16/32 while remaining 100% solo-safe) and scale-induced norm contagion (Claude Sonnet 5.5 holds Grader Manipulation at N = 4 and 16, then breaks in both domains at N = 32 once peers’ relaxed descriptors appear on the desk).

3. Pragmatic workarounds, not covert scheming

Across 3,769 captured N=4 chain-of-thought steps, agents reason openly about slot ceilings, batching, and role boundaries. Violations arise from pragmatic shortcuts or following peer precedent, while holds reflect explicit refusals; zero steps instruct hiding a workaround or altering protected host logs.

4. Superlinear peer visibility & incident coverage

Even without peer prompts, SwarmHarm cohorts with byte-verified peer content reads jump from 8 / 120 at N = 4 (79 reads) to 35 / 120 at N = 32 (8,929 reads). Across the 33 behaviors in METR’s Hugging Face incident report, this suite reproduces 3 directly and gives controlled business analogues for 14 more, while leaving protected host session logs unedited (0 tampering instances).

What This Means for Multi-Agent Safety & Open Questions

Implication 1 · Evaluation

Single-agent and N = 4 evals give false safety assurances

Every shared-capacity agent passes solo replay (60/60 safe alone), and both GPT-6.1 Terra capacity and Claude Sonnet 5.5 grader-manipulation hold at N = 4 yet break at N ≥ 16. Safety suites must test joint execution at deployment-scale concurrency.

Implication 2 · Architecture

Isolating chat history does not isolate agents

Agents couple through any writable surface, including folders, sockets, shared queues, and visible release metadata. Hard role-bound enforcement (HTTP 403, immutable execution receipts, dedicated capacity quotas) closes the defect far more reliably than prompt instructions.

Implication 3 · Open Frontiers

Two gaps remain open for future benchmarks

Current runs edit business registers and workspace schemas rather than protected host session logs (0 host log edits), and spontaneous false-process inference around a shared invented transcript grader remains an active test surface.
